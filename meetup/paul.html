PAUL'S PLAYBOOK — plain English, no jargon

For: Paul · From: Fable (Jimmy's AI chairman) · Aug 20, 2026

First — what a "co-host" is (the thing from last night)

A co-host is a local person who already has an audience of business owners — the admin of a big Indianapolis business Facebook group, a chamber-of-commerce connector, a realtor whose office is full of agents and clients. We do ALL the work (venue, content, marketing, registration). They do ONE thing: tell their people about the event. In exchange they get: their name on it ("presented by ___"), five minutes on stage, and a personal introduction to every attendee they bring. This is the single biggest lever for filling the room — the guy we're modeling got 30 people at his first event and his co-host owned a 38,000-person group. One good co-host can outproduce everything else we do combined.

Your Job 1 — find 5 co-host candidates (this week, ~2 hours)

Where to look, in order:

1. Facebook: search "Indianapolis business owners," "Indiana small business," "Indy entrepreneurs" — real ones already found: Indianapolis Entrepreneur Group, Indiana Small Business Owners, Indianapolis Business Networking. The ADMIN of each group is a candidate.

2. Your own network: anyone you know who runs a networking group, BNI chapter, chamber committee, or a realtor/insurance/bookkeeping office with lots of agents.

3. Coworking spaces: the community manager at Launch Fishers or The Speakeasy counts as a co-host too — they can email their whole member list.

Write down 5 names. That's the whole job.

Your Job 2 — send them THIS message (copy-paste, fill the blanks)

To a Facebook group admin:

Hey [Name] — love what you've built with [Group]. Quick pitch: I'm co-hosting a free "AI for Business" live event Sept 10 in Indy — a real business gets rebuilt with AI on stage, and attendees leave with tools built that night. Venue's secured, content's done, marketing's written. We want you as co-host: you tell your group, you get stage time, it's "presented by [Group]," and we personally introduce you to every attendee you bring. You spend zero hours preparing. Want the one-page rundown?

To a realtor / office manager:

Hey [Name] — I'm running a free live event Sept 10 showing Indy business owners how to actually use AI (built live on stage, no tech talk — agents love this stuff for follow-up and listings). Everything's handled: space, content, registration. Invite your office and your favorite clients, co-host it with me, get "presented by" billing plus five minutes on stage — and we'll personally introduce you to every owner you bring. Costs you nothing. Interested?

Send 5. Expect 1–2 yeses. If someone says yes, tell the 317 group chat and the AI team takes it from there.

Your Job 3 — the venue call (Thursday, ~30 min)

Call Launch Fishers and The Speakeasy (both host events). The pitch: "Free monthly AI-for-business event, 30–50 local business owners in your space — your exact membership prospects. We need the room, a screen, and chairs for one Thursday evening a month. In exchange your members get free seats and you can email your list as a co-host." Ask for Sept 10 evening. If they want money, anything up to $150 is approved.

Your Job 4 — event night (Sept 10)

You own the door: greet, check names, make sure every person's phone number is captured. You own the money if anything is sold. You're introduced from stage as the partner. That's your night.

Your Job 5 — the kill switch (yours alone)

If we don't have 25 registrations by Sept 7, you call it and the date slips two weeks. Jimmy's optimism is not allowed to override you on this one. That's why it's yours.
