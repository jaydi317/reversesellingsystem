# How the letter changed

This is an editorial history for Paul, Jason, Daegan, and Fable. The changes are reasons to test the new version, not measured conversion gains.

## What stayed

The headline Jimmy loved: **Come watch AI run a business. Maybe yours.** The hand-drawn humor and copper color. Real business work on the big screen. A free seat, food, and parking. Mom's exact testimonial. One registration goal. Jimmy and Paul as full partners.

## Original page: the foundation

The original established the live event, local details, AI helpers, and the doodle language. It made a useful invitation possible. Its historical copy is retained in the review hub so nobody has to rely on a summary of what came before.

## Jason's rebuild: make the offer tangible

Jimmy singled out Jason's timer, calculator, and clearer presentation of the take-home materials. Those ideas became useful inputs. V3 counts down to the event start, rather than inventing a registration deadline. The calculator uses the owner's own numbers and states what they mean. The take-homes say what an owner can do, without made-up dollar values.

## Astra V1: give the letter a voice

The first Astra candidate used direct, short sentences, after-hours recognition, early honesty about the optional paid relationship, and humor. The line “Your business has an after-hours department. It's you.” gave the problem a human shape. “If the answer is junk, we don't clap for it” showed judgment instead of AI worship.

## Astra V2: connect the letter to a useful night

Paul's logistics story supplied actual experience. The customer-first demonstration showed how a better question changes the work. The take-homes became original tools, exercises, skills, notes, and a room prototype that could be tried. Paul gained real stage responsibilities.

But the working audience frame still gave too much weight to relief from admin and to owners who did not know where to start.

## Jimmy's correction: the invitation has to travel

The first registrations came mainly from Paul's warm outreach. Their answers were influenced by that invitation and the registration question. They are useful for preparing to help those individuals. They are not a representative survey of the owners who will arrive from Facebook groups and ads.

V3 is written for people who have built something good and want a stronger business. AI experience varies. They may want more of the right customers, clearer messages, room to grow, or less work between them and the work they love.

## Updated Astra V3: the order now does the persuading

| Change | Before | V3 | Why it may help |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Relief and busywork carried much of the case. | “You can be damn good at what you do and still wish more people knew it.” | Makes the invitation relevant to ambition as well as exhaustion. |
| First demonstration | Admin recognition came early. | The customer is asking one thing; the website is answering another. | Lets the reader notice a marketing problem before being told about a method. |
| Paul | Biography plus trusted partner. | Real story, opening the room, owner interviews, practical demonstration, shared decisions. | Equal partnership is visible in the actual work. |
| AI promise | Big promise of helpers could be read as installed automation. | First drafts, reusable instructions, checks, and separate setup for integrations. | Owners can tell what they will actually receive. |
| Live promise | “Maybe yours” could sound like every business gets built on stage. | Selected businesses go on screen; everyone can try the exercises. | Sets a promise that a two-hour event can reasonably keep. |
| Calculator | Time savings could dominate the argument. | Comes after the growth example and practical helper. Uses personal time value, not promised income. | Adds participation without defining the whole audience as overwhelmed beginners. |
| Close | Attend an AI event. | “Give the business you believe in two hours.” | Brings the choice back to the owner's business and its possibilities. |

## What still needs human judgment

Read V3 out loud. Mark any line Jimmy would never say. Check where attention drops. Have Paul choose and rehearse his demonstration. Verify the event promises against the actual take-home experience. Use feedback from intended owners outside the existing warm network before declaring any version the winner.
