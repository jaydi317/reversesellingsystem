# One-bottleneck review

Begin with the owner's actual words and last busy day. If the problem is vague, ask one question at a time about repeated typing, waiting, handoffs, or work deferred until after hours. Four useful answers usually suffice for a first experiment.

Name up to three candidate tasks. Compare frequency, total time including review, consequence of error, source quality, and ease of checking. Unknown facts remain unknown. Separate measured values from owner estimates and model hypotheses. Do not translate every hour into cash saved or infer private profitability from public websites.

Recommend one reversible experiment that can begin with synthetic or de-identified data. State the input, draft output, human check, manual fallback, and stop condition. Name one job to leave with a person. Start with existing tools when they are actually available; verify current access and cost before recommending a purchase or migration.

For an operator brief, include the owner's problem, evidence, one proposed workflow, prerequisites, acceptance test, and remaining questions. Do not invent packages, audit prices, refund terms, or promises. A briefing is not an installed automation. Sending messages, changing records, making payments, or connecting accounts requires authorization for that action.
